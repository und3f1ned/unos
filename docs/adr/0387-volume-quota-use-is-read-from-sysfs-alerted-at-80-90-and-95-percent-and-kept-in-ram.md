# ADR-0387: A volume's quota use is read from the kernel's sysfs, alerted once per level of 80, 90 and 95 percent, and its hourly history is kept in RAM and written at shutdown

## Context

`storage.volume-quota-usage` asks that the Volumes dialog show each volume's use against its quota, that crossing 80, 90 or 95 percent send an alert, and that a graph show the use over time. A volume's quota is a limit on the referenced bytes of its quota group 0/<id> (ADR-0069, ADR-0243), which only pools with quotas enabled have. Every periodic read must leave a sleeping disk asleep (ADR-0378), nothing may be written periodically to the system disk (ADR-0214), and the owner delegated the choices, asking above all that it be safe (ADR-0013).

## Decision

- **The read.** The kernel exports each quota group in sysfs (Linux 5.9 and later; the files below checked in `fs/btrfs/sysfs.c` of 6.1, the dev device's kernel): `/sys/fs/btrfs/<fsid>/qgroups/0_<id>/referenced`, `max_referenced` and `limit_flags`, whose bit 0 (`BTRFS_QGROUP_LIMIT_MAX_RFER`) says that the group has a limit on its referenced bytes, and `qgroups/inconsistent` (6.1), 1 while btrfs's counts need a rescan. `supervisor/src/storage/qgroups.py` reads them in the supervisor's container, which has the host's sysfs, as `offsite/service.py` reads `/sys/fs/btrfs`: counters in the kernel's memory, read without a process, an ioctl or a disk read, a few small files per volume however many snapshots the pool has. `btrfs qgroup show` is not used: it walks the whole quota tree, one group per snapshot, and btrfs-progs 6 resolves each group's path. The use is the referenced bytes, which the quota limits (data shared with the volume's snapshots counts too); a limit on the exclusive bytes (`btrfs qgroup limit -e`), which UNOS never sets, is no quota here, and a limit of 0 bytes is a full quota. A volume's state is `counted`, `inconsistent` (counted, but btrfs says a rescan is needed: `btrfs quota rescan <mountpoint>`), `quotas_off` (no `qgroups` folder: quotas are off on the pool, and nothing is counted) or `unknown` (its group's files cannot be read, or its filesystem's UUID is not one, so that the kernel has no folder of it).
- **The dialog.** `GET /api/v1/storage/pools/{pool}/volumes` adds `used`, `quota` (null without a limit) and `usage` (the state) to each volume, from the names and ids it reads already. The dialog shows the use against the quota with a bar and the percentage (its colour a warning from 80% and an error from 95%, as the alerts), the use alone for a volume without a quota, and one note for quotas that are off (with what they cost, slower snapshot deletion and balance, and `btrfs quota enable`) or counts that need a rescan.
- **Sampling.** At each pass of the RAID monitor (every 5 minutes, ADR-0332), from the pools that its pass read (`pools_and_managed_names(scan=False)`, no disk scan), `supervisor/src/storage/volume_usage.py` reads the use of the volumes of every mounted pool with quotas on. Which subvolume id is which volume comes from the monitor's last list of the pool's volumes (`pools.mounted_pool_volumes`: `btrfs inspect-internal rootid`, then the list and byte-for-byte check of ADR-0069), listed again when `/sys/fs/btrfs/<fsid>/generation` (6.1 has it), the number of the filesystem's running transaction, changed since, and once more at the next pass: a create, delete or rename of a volume and a change of a limit all happen in a transaction, but one that already ran when the first list read its number leaves the number as it was, and btrfs commits a transaction within 30 seconds, long before the next pass (`_Listed.settled`). A pool that nothing wrote to costs sysfs reads alone; the lists run in the passes after a write and read the metadata that the write, and then the first list, just read, from the page cache. A list that fails (a pool mounted at another subvolume, btrfs failing or not answering) is tried once more and then after the next write, and logged once; a volume of the last list whose id btrfs cannot resolve fails the list, rather than counting as deleted. Without `generation` (an older kernel) every pass lists the volumes. The lists and their generations are kept with the history, so a restart of the supervisor lists nothing new; the list of a pool that the RAID monitor no longer finds at all goes.
- **The levels.** Fixed at 80, 90 and 95 percent (`QUOTA_LEVELS`), as the entry names them and as the pool's levels are fixed (ADR-0332): not settable, which would need a setting, its page and its writes. A volume with a quota reaches a level at `used >= level% of the quota` and leaves it below `level - 5`%, so that one that hovers at a level is alerted once.
- **The alerts.** A problem of the source `volumes` per volume above a level (`health.problems`, ADR-0332): key `volume_quota:<pool>/<subvolume id>`, kind `volume_quota_80`, `_90` or `_95` (the banner's text), subject `<pool>/<volume>`, a warning at 80 and 90 and an error at 95. `Problem.stage` (the level, not sent to the web UI) makes `problems.update` alert a problem again, with the time of that update, when its stage rises: one alert per level crossed upward, one only for the highest when a pass finds several crossed at once, nothing when the use falls, and a level alerted again after the use fell below its clearing point. Under 75% the problem is gone. A volume whose use cannot be read or is counted inconsistently, and the volumes of a pool whose list fails or whose mount could not be read (the host's mount table, health unknown), keep the problems found before; those of a pool that is not mounted or has quotas off go. Inconsistent counts feed neither the problems nor the history: during a `btrfs quota rescan` started on inconsistent counts the kernel counts again from 0, and the volume keeps its problem and gets no point until it ends. A rescan started on consistent counts shows nothing in 6.1's sysfs (its RESCAN flag is not exported): the use then falls to 0 and back, the problem goes, and comes back with an alert, as after a deletion and a refill. Each volume's stage is kept with its history and written at shutdown, so that a start keeps the level that a volume reached (87% after 90% stays at 90); the problem itself is new to the RAM of the new start and so alerted once more, as every problem is after a restart (ADR-0332).
- **The history.** One point per volume per clock hour (the first pass in each hour: the time, the referenced bytes and the quota), for 30 days (at most 720 points), in RAM, keyed by the pool's fsid and the subvolume id: a rename keeps it, a volume that a list no longer has loses it, and points older than 30 days go, also those of a pool that is no longer mounted. It is written to `/data/volume_usage.json` (`HISTORY_FILE`) only at shutdown (`stop_health_monitors`, as ADR-0269 writes the SMART history), and only when RAM holds something the file does not, and read at the first use after a start; a file that cannot be read as the history is set aside as `.bad` (ADR-0324), also one with a name, a UUID, an id or a stage that the API would refuse (its names reach the alerts and the web UI) or a time that cannot be shown, and one that cannot be opened is read again at the next pass and never written over. A point ahead of the clock (a pass with a clock set ahead, or the file) is dropped at the next pass and at a start, so the history goes on once the clock is right. `GET /api/v1/storage/pools/{pool}/volumes/{name}/usage` answers the points, the current reading last, and the Volumes dialog draws them on demand ("Use over time").
- **A deleted subvolume's quota group.** Linux 6.1 keeps the quota group 0/<id> of a deleted subvolume, with its limit, and a subvolume that btrfs later gives the same id (the highest one, after a remount) would meet it: the limit, or a create with a quota refused (ADR-0352). The kernel (6.1.y's `btrfs_remove_qgroup`) refuses to destroy the group as busy while it counts bytes or holds reservations, until btrfs has dropped the subvolume's data and committed its counts: the dev device's kernel (6.1.172) refused it right after every delete on 2026-11-17, and destroyed it once `btrfs subvolume sync` and `btrfs filesystem sync` had run. So the Delete leaves the group, and the monitor's lists destroy the groups whose subvolume is gone: those of deleted volumes, restored copies (ADR-0358) and snapshots (`_destroy_stale_groups` of `volume_usage.py`, `destroy_quota_group` of `supervisor/src/storage/pools.py`: `btrfs qgroup destroy 0/<id> <mountpoint>` under the host's `timeout` of 60 s, ADR-0258). A group is destroyed only when two lists in a row found it without its subvolume, its ids read from sysfs (`qgroups/0_<id>`, the top level's 0/5 left out) before each list, and only once sysfs reads 0 for its `referenced`, `exclusive` and `rsv_*` counts: a subvolume that one list missed (6.1 makes a new subvolume's group, counting 0, before its root item) keeps its group, and a group that still counts gets no command. Every destroy starts a transaction, refused or not, which moves the generation and brings two more lists; a group that still counts therefore waits for a write to the pool and is never retried in a loop that would keep the disks awake, and one that three destroys left is given up with one warning that names the command to run by hand. A destroy that does not answer ends the pass's destroys. A stale group that still counts while btrfs's counts need a rescan never reaches 0 (the kernel then counts nothing): it is logged once with `btrfs quota rescan <mountpoint>`. After a deletion the group goes at the second list after the drop's commit, about 5 to 10 minutes; it stays while the monitor does not run or the pool is not mounted, and a start of the supervisor needs two lists, which a write brings, before it destroys anything.
- **Roles.** Both routes are in the storage router, which is for administrators only (`_ADMIN_ONLY` of `supervisor/src/main.py`, ADR-0030); the problems reach administrators only, over the events socket.
- Tests: `supervisor/tests/test_volume_usage.py`, the problems' stage in `supervisor/tests/test_alert_events.py`, the pass with every disk asleep in `supervisor/tests/test_disk_spindown.py`, the Delete leaving the quota group in `supervisor/tests/test_volumes.py`, the file in `supervisor/tests/test_state_files.py`, `webui/tests/volume-usage.spec.ts`.

## Consequences

- No write to the system disk while the supervisor runs. Each stop writes the file once, about 40 bytes per point: at most about 30 KB per volume, 0.3 MiB for 10 volumes of 30 days, and nothing when no hour passed and no list changed. A power cut or a crash loses the points since the last stop: the graph shows a gap.
- A volume that fills faster than 5 minutes per level is alerted at its highest level at once, and a full volume refuses writes by itself (btrfs's quota): the alert is for slow filling.
- A pool written to all the time is listed at every pass, 3 host commands each 5 minutes; a pool written once, at two passes, and a deletion with quotas on adds a destroy of its group and two more lists. A subvolume id that btrfs gives a new volume after another was deleted (btrfs can reuse the highest id after a remount) inherits the deleted volume's history only when no pass saw the deletion: the supervisor stopped or the pool unmounted meanwhile.
- A host kernel older than 5.9 shows every volume's use as quotas off, one older than 6.1 counts that need a rescan as counted.
- A clock set back adds no point until it passes the newest; a pass with a clock set far ahead drops the points older than 30 days before that time, and its own point goes once the clock is right. The counting cost of quotas on a pool with many snapshots (slower snapshot deletion and balance) stays the administrator's choice, made when enabling them; UNOS still does not enable quotas (ADR-0243).

## Considered options

- `btrfs qgroup show --raw` at every pass: a process per pool, a walk of every quota group, and tree reads that can reach a sleeping disk once its metadata left the page cache.
- The history in SQLite, a row per volume per hour: about 0.5 MiB a day on the system disk, a periodic write that ADR-0214 rules out. On the pool: it would wake its disk every hour (ADR-0378).
- RAM only, lost at every restart: every update of UNOS would empty the graph.
- Settable levels per volume or for all: a setting written to the system disk and a page for it, for levels the entry fixes.
