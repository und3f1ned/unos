# ADR-0107: SMB accounts use uids 70000-79999 and gid 70000

## Context

The uids and the gid of SMB accounts end up in the ownership of files on the pools, so no other account may ever get one of them. The first range, 20000-29999 (ADR-0106), lies below `UID_MAX` and `GID_MAX` of login.defs, so a hand-made `useradd` or `groupadd` can pick one of its ids, and an account made by hand could take a deleted UNOS user's uid and that user's files.

## Decision

- `SMB_UID_MIN` and `SMB_UID_MAX` are 70000 and 79999, and the group unos-smb has `SMB_GROUP_GID` 70000 (`supervisor/src/shares/constants.py`). The CHECK constraint of `smb_accounts.uid`, the texts and the examples follow.
- The range lies above `UID_MAX` and `GID_MAX` of login.defs (60000), so a hand-made `useradd` or `groupadd` never picks one; below `SUB_UID_MIN` (100000); and in a block that systemd never allocates (65536-524287).
- When no uid is left, an activation answers 409 "No SMB user ids left (70000-79999)".
- Tests: `supervisor/tests/test_smb_models.py` (`TestConstants`, `TestConstraints`).

## Consequences

- The range holds 10,000 uids, and uids are never handed out twice (ADR-0109).
- Apps that run as uid 1000 never collide with an SMB account.
- A host group that already has gid 70000 blocks new share folders, Repair permissions and new SMB accounts until it is removed or renumbered (ADR-0151).

## Considered options

- 20000-29999 and gid 20000 (ADR-0106): superseded.
