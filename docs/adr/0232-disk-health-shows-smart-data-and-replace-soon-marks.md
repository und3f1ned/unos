# ADR-0232: Disk Health opens each disk's SMART data, and disks whose SMART trend scores 50 are marked replace soon

## Context

Disk Health listed each disk's model, temperature and health, but none of the SMART attributes that `GET /api/v1/health/disks` returns (storage.smart). The SMART monitor keeps the critical attributes of every awake disk per serial in `/data/smart_history.json`, and `GET /api/v1/health/smart/predictions` scored that history, but no page read the scores and no ADR fixed how they are computed (storage.smart-replace-soon). The score's rate was taken over the whole history, so a month of quiet history hid a fast rise, and the history keeps the serials of removed and replaced disks, whose predictions never changed.

## Decision

- Disk Health (`webui/src/routes/system/tabs/HealthTab.svelte`) opens each disk in a dialog with the data that `GET /health/disks` returned, so opening a disk runs no smartctl: overall health, temperature, power-on hours, power cycles, serial, firmware, capacity, the last self-test (the newest entry of the ATA self-test log, or of the NVMe one that smartctl 7.3 and later print) and the ATA attributes with their status (Failing at or below the attribute's threshold, Warning within 10 of it). NVMe disks have no ATA attributes: `SmartInfo.nvme_health` carries the numbers of their SMART/Health Information log in smartctl's order, and the dialog lists them. A disk without SMART data says so.
- The score (`predict_disk_failure` in `supervisor/src/health/router.py`) keeps the endpoint's rules, except that the rate is taken over the last day. A disk needs 24 snapshots, a day of hourly passes. Each counter is read from the snapshots that hold it: one without it (smartctl could not read the attribute table, and the monitor stored none) is skipped, not read as 0. Over its history, at most 720 snapshots (30 days), each of Reallocated_Sector_Ct (5), Current_Pending_Sector (197) and Offline_Uncorrectable (198) that rose adds 30, and 20 more when it rose by more than 0.1 per snapshot over its last 24 snapshots; each of Reported_Uncorrect (187), Command_Timeout (188) and UDMA_CRC_Error_Count (199) that rose above 100 adds 10. 50 or more (`REPLACE_SOON_SCORE`) is replace_soon, 30 monitor_closely, 10 watch, less healthy. Each addition to the score adds a risk factor, the reason shown. A disk whose history holds none of these attributes (NVMe disks: the monitor records ATA attributes only) gets no prediction, and neither do the disks that smartctl reports without a serial, which the monitor records together under "unknown".
- Predictions cover the disks connected now, the disks of Disk Health (ADR-0072). The monitor stores lsblk's serial with each snapshot (`lsblk_serial`, which can differ from smartctl's behind a USB bridge), and a history goes by its latest snapshot. A history whose lsblk serial no connected disk reports belongs to no disk. One whose lsblk serial exactly one connected disk reports and no other history holds belongs to that disk, at its path now. Any other history (without an lsblk serial, as behind a bridge that reports none; or with one that several connected disks report or several histories hold: identical USB bridges, a disk replaced inside its enclosure) belongs to the disk now at the path of its latest snapshot, if that disk reports the history's lsblk serial when it has one and no history claims the disk by its lsblk serial. Of several histories at one path, the later latest snapshot wins. The endpoint lists the disks with lsblk on the host in a worker thread, reads the history and runs no smartctl; it answers 500 when the host's disks cannot be listed, like the other SMART endpoints, and when the history cannot be read.
- A disk is marked replace soon while its prediction says replace_soon, and nothing about the mark is stored. Disk Health marks the disk whose serial (smartctl's, in the list and in the history) is the prediction's, with the risk factors and the recommendation, and lists a replace_soon prediction that matches no listed disk in an alert with its path, serial and risk factors: smartctl could not read the disk on this load (its serial shows as Unknown), or another disk took its place. The dashboard shows administrators an alert that lists each such disk with its path, serial and risk factors, and links to Disk Health. Both pages read the predictions at every load and refresh, so a mark goes with the first load after its status changed. When the predictions cannot be read, both pages say so instead of showing no mark.
- The monitor writes the history file atomically: staged next to it, synced, then renamed over it, so that a reader or a power loss finds the old history or the new one, never a part of it. A file that cannot be read, or holds no snapshots by serial, is not an empty history: the SMART history endpoints answer 500 with the reason, and the monitor's next pass logs it and starts a new history, so that a damaged file does not stop the recording.
- Tests: `supervisor/tests/test_smart_predictions.py` (the score, its threshold, the window, the disks covered and the history file), `supervisor/tests/test_health_smart.py` (the attributes, the NVMe log and the last self-test of the dialog), `webui/tests/health.spec.ts`, `webui/tests/dashboard.spec.ts` (on the route fakes of `webui/tests/health-fakes.ts`, which give only answers the server can give) and `webui/tests/roles.spec.ts` (no prediction request for regular users).

## Consequences

- The score is crude. It follows rises, not levels: a disk that arrives with many reallocated sectors gets no mark. A mark lasts while the score is 50 or more, for example while a sector count rose by 3 or more within its last 24 snapshots (more than a day for a disk that sleeps), or while two sector counts rose within the kept history; a single rise leaves the mark 24 snapshots after it, as monitor closely. A better model needs an ADR of its own.
- NVMe disks are never marked: their failing state shows only as FAILED health (smartctl reads the controller's critical warnings) and in the numbers of their health log, without a status.
- A disk that sleeps is not recorded (`smartctl -n standby`) and keeps the prediction of its last snapshots. Disk Health itself still wakes it, because `GET /health/disks` runs smartctl without `-n standby` (storage.disk-spindown).
- Snapshots taken before `lsblk_serial` was stored, and disks behind a bridge that reports no serial, or a serial that another disk reports or another history holds, are placed by path: until the monitor records the disk that took over such a path, the dashboard lists the old serial there, and Disk Health lists it apart from its disks. A disk replaced inside the same USB enclosure keeps the old disk's mark until the monitor has recorded the new disk: lsblk reports the bridge's serial for both, at the same path, so neither the serial nor the path tells them apart. The latest snapshots compare as local-time text, so after the clock went back (a board without a real-time clock before NTP, the end of daylight saving time) an older history can win such a tie for a while.
- A history file damaged from outside UNOS loses its snapshots: the endpoints answer 500 until the monitor's next pass, at most an hour later, and the marks come back only after 24 new snapshots. The atomic write adds a sync and a rename to the hourly write, not more bytes.
- Each load of Disk Health lists the host's disks twice, once for each request: lsblk reads sysfs and writes nothing.
- The web UI is tested on route fakes, as the stand-in has no disks; the backend tests pin the texts that the fakes copy.
- No mark can be dismissed. The monitor's SMART trend alerts by e-mail and Telegram stay as they are (storage.smart-trend-alerts).
