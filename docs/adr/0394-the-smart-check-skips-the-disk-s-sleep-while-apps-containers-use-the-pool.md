# ADR-0394: The smart check skips the disk's sleep while the containers of apps use it, and names them

## Context

`scripts/dev/scenario.sh smart` waits for the pool's disk to sleep with the shortest idle timeout (ADR-0378's device check) and fails the step when the disk is still awake after 1980 s. Since Immich's four containers, transmission and others run on the dev device with their data on the pool `data` (ADR-0381 names that cost), the disk rarely sleeps: after the seventh batch's reboot `scenario.sh all` passed 185 steps and failed only that one, "active after 1980 s; meanwhile the kernel counted 13 reads and 649 writes", a failure that is expected and hides a real fault of `storage.disk-spindown`. On the device (Debian 13, kernel 6.1.172, Docker with the systemd cgroup driver on cgroup v2) each container's cgroup counts the reads and writes its processes send each disk, in `io.stat` by the disk's major:minor: in two minutes of the idle device Immich's database sent `sda` (8:0) 22 writes, and `sda` completed 42. The two are not the same unit (the cgroup counts what is sent, the disk what it completed after merging), and what the kernel's own threads send, such as btrfs's commits, counts in no container.

## Decision

- **Evidence, read in each round of the wait.** Before the wait and after each of its rounds (the timeout and 30 s, 330 s), before that round's power read, and once more after the last power read of a disk not asleep (an app's use in the moment after that read counts too, though it cannot have made the disk active at it), the check reads the disk's reads and writes (`/sys/block/<disk>/stat`, as before) and each running container's (`Device.container_io`, `REMOTE_CONTAINER_IO` of `scripts/dev/scenario.py`): `docker ps` for each container's ID and name (under the host's `timeout`, 30 s), then `docker inspect` of each ID for its first process (10 s each, and none after 120 s from the script's start, so that it ends within `scenario.Device`'s 300 s; no container is started, ADR-0183), its cgroup from `/proc/<pid>/cgroup` (the `0::` line of cgroup v2) and `rios=` and `wios=` of the disk's line of `/sys/fs/cgroup/<cgroup>/io.stat` (no line: 0 and 0). Every container is known by the name that `docker ps` gives it; one without a PID from `inspect` (it ended since `ps`, dockerd failed or did not answer in time), without a process (not running) or without an `io.stat` has no count in that reading and counts for nothing between it and another, and a `docker ps` that fails makes the whole reading unknown. It runs in the read-only mount namespace (ADR-0221) and reads no disk. A container whose counts fell, one started again with a new cgroup, counts from 0 (`container_io_moved`); one started again whose new counts are above its old ones is counted short, and one that ran only between two readings is not seen.
- **SKIP, not FAIL, when the apps used the disk to the end.** When the Storage page still shows the disk `active` after the wait and, in its last two rounds (`SPINDOWN_QUIET_ROUNDS`, 660 s: longer than the disk needs unused to sleep, its timeout of 300 s or the dev device's disk's own minimum of about 600 s), the kernel counted reads or writes on it and the cgroups of containers other than UNOS's own counted some too, while UNOS's own counted none, the step is a SKIP that names those containers with their counts in those 660 s and says to stop them for the step. UNOS's own containers are the supervisor's rule (`is_unos_container`, as `is_system_container` of `supervisor/src/containers/docker.py`: every `unos-*`, among them `unos-supervisor` and `unos-webui` of `deploy/docker-compose.yml`; a test compares them). The next step, the SMART reads of the sleeping disk, is a SKIP too (the disk did not sleep), and the check goes on: the timeout is set as found, the self-tests run, and the check passes with those two SKIPs. A step can now end as a SKIP from inside (`Skipped`, which `Check.step` prints as a SKIP line and counts).
- **FAIL as before** in every other case of a disk not asleep after the wait: a state other than `active` (`unknown`, a disk not listed); no read or write counted on the disk in those 660 s (a command that restarts its timer, its own automatic offline data collection, which the step then reads, or a minimum longer than that; the step says so); reads or writes counted while no app's container counted any in them (a share, a program on the host, UNOS itself); reads or writes of UNOS's own containers in them, also beside the apps' (the step names both: the apps do not explain UNOS's own use, a fault or a task that the owner scheduled, such as an hourly snapshot, which ADR-0378 lets wake the disks it needs); and a reading of the containers that failed, or counts that could not be read, which the step names: without them the check cannot tell the apps' use from UNOS's. Apps that used the disk only earlier in the wait do not make a SKIP. One case of a disk without a read or write in those 660 s is no fault: the hourly SMART monitor reads an awake disk that was used since its last read, which restarts its timer once (ADR-0378), and a pass of it soon after the apps' last use leaves the disk awake past the wait. The check cannot see the monitor's passes, so when the apps used the disk in the 660 s before those, the FAIL says that such a pass restarts the timer once and that a check that passes when run again shows it was that; a SKIP there would hide the faults that keep the disk awake without a read or write.
- **The SMART reads of the sleeping disk.** A read that finds the disk awake (Storage's `power_state` active, Disk Health's PASSED or FAILED, the Storage page's active) or reads or writes counted during the step are a SKIP that names the containers of apps when, since the readings taken before the wait's last power read, the kernel counted reads or writes on the disk and those containers did too, and UNOS's own did not: one of them may have woken it. Every other failure of the step (an answer of the API that fails, a wrong field, an unknown state, UNOS's own containers' use, which the step names) fails it as before.
- This changes ADR-0378's device check, whose wait step failed every disk still awake after 1980 s. Tests: `supervisor/tests/test_dev_scenarios.py` (the fake's containers with their counts; the fault of a power read that restarts the timer, and the scans turned on again, still fail while the apps wrote only early in the wait; UNOS's own writes fail beside the apps'; a container without an answer at the window's start counts for nothing; an app that wrote just before the last power read is a SKIP; one that went quiet before a SMART monitor's read fails with that cause named; `REMOTE_CONTAINER_IO` run on stand-ins of sysfs, procfs, docker and timeout).

## Consequences

- While apps use the pool, `scenario.sh smart` (and `all`) passes with these two SKIPs and still waits the whole 1980 s: it cannot see whether UNOS lets the disk sleep. `storage.disk-spindown`'s sleep is shown on the device only in a run whose apps leave the disk alone for its last 660 s, or with them stopped (an owner's choice, as the Apps page's Stop). A run in which the apps went quiet early enough passes those steps, as before.
- A run in which the apps went quiet between 1320 s and 660 s before the end of the wait and a pass of the SMART monitor read the disk soon after fails, with that cause named: a false failure that a second run clears, accepted so that a SKIP does not hide a fault there. A task that the owner scheduled and that writes the pool during the wait fails the step, as before this decision.
- While an app uses the disk in the last 660 s, the SKIP hides every fault of UNOS that would keep the disk awake and that UNOS's own containers' counts do not show: writes that the kernel's threads send for it (btrfs's commits after an ioctl of the supervisor, say), and commands without a read or write (an IDENTIFY that restarts the timer, the drive's own scans turned on again). The SKIP says that the check could not judge, not that UNOS is right. The kernel's own writes are attributed to no container, so the check takes any read or write of an app's container as the cause and does not require the apps' counts to add up to the disk's.
- The check reads the device up to 14 more times in a full wait (each round's reading and the one after the last power read, a reading of the disk and one of the containers each), each a login of `scripts/dev/ssh.sh`; the step's SD MiB shows what they write.
- A container started by hand, outside UNOS's App Store, counts as an app unless its name starts with `unos-`.
- Where the containers' cgroups have no `io.stat` (cgroup v1, or the io controller off), the step fails as before with that reason.

## Considered options

- A SKIP before the wait whenever a container with a mount on the pool runs: no 33-minute wait, but the disk's sleep would never be checked on the dev device while Immich is installed, even when its containers leave the disk alone, and an idle app would hide a fault of UNOS.
- A SKIP for an app's read or write anywhere in the wait (this ADR's first version, refuted by its review): an app that wrote once in the first round hid a fault that kept the disk awake for the 1650 s after it. A window of 1320 s, room for one pass of the SMART monitor after the apps' last use: it hid a fault that restarts the timer in a run whose apps went quiet 990 s before the end, which passes without the fault.
- Reading the SMART monitor's passes: the supervisor keeps them in RAM, and its history file is written only when a snapshot changed it (ADR-0269).
- Leaving the FAIL and stopping the apps for each run: an owner action each time, and the failure that is expected hides the next real one in `scenario.sh all`.
