# ADR-0407: The power settings shut the host down at a set time or when idle, and arm the RTC to wake it

## Context

`PUT /api/v1/power/config` stored a daily shutdown time, a wake time and an idle shutdown, but nothing acted on them and no page showed them (`system.scheduled-power`). The owner delegated the decisions and asked for safety first: nothing may shut a host down unless a person set a time on purpose, a running job postpones a scheduled shutdown, every shutdown is announced to the notification channels and in a banner some minutes before, a host whose RTC cannot wake it promises no wake, times follow the host's time zone (ADR-0401, ADR-0266), and the checks write nothing to the system disk (ADR-0214).

## Decision

- **Who acts.** `supervisor/src/power/schedule.py`: `power_schedule_loop`, started and stopped in the lifespan (ADR-0043), makes a pass one second after each minute begins. It acts only on what an administrator turned on in `/data/power_config.json` through `PUT /power/config`: `auto_shutdown`, `auto_wakeup`, `idle_shutdown`, all off by default and in a damaged file (ADR-0324). With `UNOS_HOST_EXEC=local` (the stand-in, the test stack) a pass runs no host command, `GET /power/schedule` answers 503 with `POWER_OFF_DETAIL`, and `PUT /power/config` refuses with 503 settings that turn any of the three on (ADR-0233).
- **The shutdown** is that of `POST /power/shutdown` (ADR-0233): `shutdown -h +1` with a wall message (`UNOS: scheduled shutdown (power settings)`, `UNOS: idle shutdown (power settings)`) on the host through `src/host.py`, so logind lists it in the Power tab, whose Cancel stops it in its last minute; a cancelled one is not issued again that day. It is written to the audit log as `system_shutdown` by `system`, severity warning. None is issued while logind has a reboot or shutdown pending, or while that cannot be read. The UPS monitor's own shutdown, which never runs in the container, is left to `system.ups-monitoring`.
- **Times.** Hours 0 to 23, minutes 0 to 59, the idle time 20 to 1440 minutes (422 otherwise). The shutdown and wake times are on the clock of the host's time zone, as the scheduled tasks are (`scheduler.router.schedule_zone`, ADR-0266): a time that the start of daylight saving time skips comes at the end of the skip, one that its end shows twice comes the first time (`next_instant`). With both on, the wake time must come at least 10 minutes (`MIN_OFF`) after the shutdown time on that clock (400). The API answers instants in UTC with `Z` (ADR-0401) and the zone's name.
- **A scheduled shutdown** is announced 11 minutes before its time, at the pass whose decision comes 10 minutes later (`WARNING`): a problem of the source `power`, kind `shutdown_scheduled`, with the time at which the host goes down, alerted once to every enabled notification channel and shown in the health banner (ADR-0332) until the host goes down. At the pass one minute before its time it is issued, unless something holds it:
  - a running job (below), or a scheduled task due in the minute that began or the next 2 minutes (`DUE_AHEAD`), which the scheduler's pass of the same minute may not have started yet;
  - with the wake time on, an RTC alarm that is not set to the next wake time.
  A held shutdown is postponed: the problem becomes `shutdown_postponed` with what holds it (alerted once), and each pass checks again. When nothing holds it any more, it is announced again and issued 10 minutes later unless something holds it again. It is dropped for that day 6 hours after its time (`POSTPONE_LIMIT`) and when a wake time passes while it waits. A shutdown is also issued only once its announcement has been out for 10 minutes, so a time saved, or a supervisor started, less than 10 minutes before it moves it later; it is skipped when the next wake would come within 10 minutes of the host going down.
- **Idle.** The host is idle while a pass finds none of these:
  - a web session: a signed-in WebSocket of the web UI (every page keeps `/ws/v1/events` open: `websocket.events.signed_in_sockets`);
  - a login session of the host: logind's `ListSessions`, through `busctl`, without the sessions of class `manager` or `manager-early` (per-user service managers);
  - an SMB client or an NFS client: an established TCP connection to the host's port 445 or 139, or 2049 (`ss -H -t -n state established`), by the client's address;
  - a running job: a run of a scheduled task (backups, snapshots, scrubs, custom commands), a task due as above, a backup made by hand, a restore, an install, update or rollback of an app (ADR-0408), a copy, restore or settings change of the off-device copy, a repair of a share's permissions, an update or rollback of UNOS that the supervisor runs (System > Updates), and a scrub, a balance, a replace or a device removal of btrfs: a process `btrfs [options] scrub|balance start|resume`, `replace start` or `device remove|delete`, or the kernel's `btrfs-balance` or `btrfs-devrepl` thread, seen through `pid: host`, also when started outside UNOS;
  - disk activity: more than 1 MiB read and written together per minute (`DISK_ACTIVE_BYTES`) since the last pass, on a disk of `/proc/diskstats` other than the boot SD card or eMMC and the virtual devices (`storage.disks.HIDDEN_DISK_PREFIXES`); the first pass after a start has no measurement and counts as activity.
  What cannot be read counts as activity, with the reason. The idle time runs on the monotonic clock from the first pass that found nothing and starts again at every finding, at every start of the supervisor (so after every boot), when a wake time passes and after an idle shutdown was issued. At the idle time less 10 minutes the problem `shutdown_idle` is announced; at the idle time, with the announcement out for 10 minutes, the shutdown is issued, with the wake alarm's hold and the skip of a scheduled one. After an announcement that use withdrew, the next one comes an hour later at the earliest (`IDLE_REANNOUNCE_SECONDS`), so a host used now and then is not alerted again and again.
- **A clock that is set.** When the system clock moves more than 2 minutes (`CLOCK_JUMP_SECONDS`) further or less than the monotonic clock between two passes (NTP after a boot without an RTC, a person), the schedule starts over from the new time: a shutdown it followed is dropped for that day, and the idle time starts again.
- **The wake** uses the RTC `/sys/class/rtc/rtc0` and its `wakealarm`, read and written on the host through `host.run_on_host` (`sh -c` with fixed scripts, the path and the time as arguments). The RTC can take the wake when rtc0 and its `wakealarm` exist and its clock (`since_epoch`) is within 5 minutes of the system clock (an RTC kept in local time or without a battery is not); otherwise the answer names why and promises no wake. While the wake time is on, each pass keeps the alarm at the next wake time: `0`, then the time in seconds since the epoch, when it holds another value (also after it fired), and a read back. `PUT /power/config` that turns the wake on or changes its time on a host whose RTC cannot take it answers 409 with the reason and stores nothing; otherwise it arms the alarm before it stores the settings (500 when the RTC does not keep it) and clears it again when they cannot be stored; one that turns it off clears the alarm, or stores the change alone on a host that no longer has rtc0 or its `wakealarm`. With the wake off UNOS leaves the RTC alone. Whether the board powers on from its RTC is the board's, as for Wake-on-LAN (ADR-0247): the page says so.
- **What survives.** The schedule comes from the stored settings, written only by a `PUT` that changes them. A pass writes no file: what it follows (the announcement, a postponement, the idle time, the last disk counters) is in RAM, the alarm in the RTC, a pending shutdown in logind. After a restart or a boot the next times come from the settings: a shutdown whose time has passed waits for the next day (a postponement is forgotten), the idle time starts again, and an announcement still due is alerted again (ADR-0332). Each pass reads the disk counters of `/proc/diskstats` in the container, also while the settings are off; it runs host commands only for what is on. A pass logs only a change: a shutdown issued, postponed, announced again, dropped or skipped, an alarm set, a clock set, a failure once.
- **The status.** `GET /api/v1/power/schedule` (administrators, area `power`, read only) answers the zone; the scheduled shutdown (`time`, `next`, `state` off, planned, announced, postponed or issued, `held_by`); the wake (`time`, `next`, `rtc` with `available`, `reason` and the `alarm` it holds); the idle shutdown (`minutes`, `idle`, `since`, `shutdown_at`, `found`). Each finding has a `kind`, a `subject` (a task, app, pool, address, user or disk), an `amount` (pages open, bytes) and an English `detail` for what could not be read. It reads the host and changes nothing.
- **The web UI.** System > Power's card Shutdown and wake schedule (`webui/src/routes/system/tabs/PowerTab.svelte`) edits the three settings, names the host's zone of the settings and the browser's zone of the plan's times, and shows the next shutdown, its holds, the next wake with the RTC's answer and alarm, and whether the host is idle and why not, as read when the tab opens and after a save (no polling, ADR-0365); texts in English and Russian (`catalog/power.ts`), the three problem kinds in `catalog/problems.ts`.
- **Tests:** `supervisor/tests/test_power_schedule.py` (the passes on a fake clock and a fake host: the announcement, the shutdown, each hold, the postponement and its limit, a start late, a pending action, a cancel, the changes of daylight saving time in Berlin, the alarm set, re-armed, refused and cleared, every idle finding, what cannot be read, the stand-in, no file written and nothing logged by a quiet pass), `supervisor/tests/test_power.py`, `supervisor/tests/test_lifespan.py`, `webui/tests/power.spec.ts`.

## Consequences

- A host with a connected SMB or NFS client, an SSH session or an open page of the web UI never idles; one whose apps keep the data disks busy neither. A scheduled shutdown cuts off clients that are connected but run no job: the announcement and the wall message are their warning.
- A SMART self-test running in a disk does not hold a shutdown. A job started in the minute after the shutdown was issued does not stop it, and neither does turning the daily shutdown off then: Cancel shutdown does.
- The shutdown comes at its time at the earliest, and later when held or announced late; an announcement still due is alerted again after a restart of the supervisor.
- An installation whose stored settings turned one of the three on before this decision acts on them from its first start with it: the dev device's settings are checked before the deploy (the entry's notes).
- A task whose time falls while the host is off does not run (ADR-0266): the times of the tasks and of the shutdown are the administrator's to fit together.
- Every pass while the idle shutdown is on runs `ss`, `busctl` (once, and once more per session) and reads every process's command line; while the wake is on, one `sh` that reads two sysfs files. None writes.
- Host-level: the device check arms a schedule far ahead and reads `GET /power/schedule` and the RTC's `wakealarm`, then puts the settings back; a real shutdown and wake are the owner's, because the board may not power on from its RTC.

## Considered options

- Handing the shutdown time to logind (`shutdown -h 23:00`) or a systemd timer: a job running then could not postpone it, and a file on the host would be written for each change.
- Arming the RTC only right before UNOS's own shutdowns: a host shut down by its power button or the Power tab would not wake at the set time.
- Counting only connections or only disk activity as use: a mounted share with no traffic, or a session that reads nothing, would let the host go down under a person; the owner asked for safety first.
