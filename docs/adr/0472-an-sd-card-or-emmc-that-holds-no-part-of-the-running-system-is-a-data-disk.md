# ADR-0472: An SD card or eMMC that holds no part of the running system is a data disk

## Context

UNOS hid every `mmcblk` device by its name (`HIDDEN_DISK_PREFIXES` of `supervisor/src/storage/disks.py`), as the boot medium of ARM boards (ADR-0072, ADR-0225). On 2026-11-23 the BPI-W3's system moved to its eMMC (`mmcblk0`: the root filesystem and U-Boot; ADR-0470, which supersedes ADR-0225), its SD card (`mmcblk1`, a 29.7 GB SanDisk card) was wiped and left in its slot, and the owner said to "use the SD" as the spare disk that the storage checks need: GET /storage/disks listed only `sda`. The owner delegated the decisions ("do as much as possible without me", 2026-11-22), above all safe.

## Decision

- **Which SD card or eMMC is a disk.** A whole `mmcblk<N>` device is listed on the Storage page and offered for pools like a SATA or USB disk when none of it (the card, its partitions, the LVM, LUKS or md devices on them) is part of the running system or used by the host (`disks._mmc_use`, read with the rest of `_collect_host_state`): mounted at `/`, `/boot`, `/boot/efi` or `/boot/firmware`, active swap, the device that the kernel's `root=` names, or, as Armbian's boot scripts pass it, `ubootpart=` (the PARTUUID of the partition U-Boot loaded the kernel from; it marks any disk as the system's, `_boot_specs`); mounted anywhere else, as lsblk or the host's mount table shows it (by path, device number or btrfs UUID), except at a pool's mountpoint (a folder of `pools_base_path`), so that a pool on the card keeps it listed as that pool's. A card in use stays hidden as before: never listed, offered, imported (ADR-0283) or given an idle timeout, with the reason "a boot or virtual device". Every card is hidden while what tells its use cannot be read: the mount table (also one without `/`, as from a findmnt that printed nothing) or the kernel's command line.
- **Areas of an eMMC are never disks**, by their names: `mmcblk0boot0`, `mmcblk0boot1`, `mmcblk0rpmb` and the general purpose partitions `mmcblk0gp<N>` (`disks.is_hidden_disk`); the validators accept only `mmcblk<N>` and `mmcblk<N>p<M>` (`MMC_DISK_PATTERN` of `supervisor/src/security/validators.py`).
- **Its identity** comes from the host's sysfs where it has one (`host.block_device_attribute`, read with lsblk's tree in `disks._lsblk_tree`, so that every reader and the identity check of a pool's creation see the same): `device/name` as its model (lsblk reports none), `device/serial` as its serial (lsblk reports udev's `<name>_<serial>`), and `device/type` as `mmc_type` of GET /storage/disks: `SD`, `MMC` (shown as eMMC) or `unknown`; null for every other disk.
- **No SMART.** smartctl has none for mmc devices and never runs on one: GET /storage/disks/{name}/smart answers `healthy` null and the power state active; Disk Health lists the card with the health status `NO_SMART` ("No SMART"), lsblk's model, serial and size, and its dialog says why; GET /health/overview counts it in `no_smart`; a self-test, manual or scheduled, is refused with 400 (`disks.smart_disk_device`), and the Scheduler offers no SD card or eMMC for one; the hourly SMART monitor passes over it.
- **No spin-down.** `disks.power_state` answers active without hdparm or smartctl, so a pool on the card is never taken for a sleeping one; `spindown.problem` refuses an idle timeout with 409 ("an SD card or eMMC, which has no disk that spins down").
- **It wears.** The Storage page shows its type (SD card, eMMC) in the Type column, and the Create pool dialog says, while an SD card or eMMC is selected, "An SD card or eMMC wears with writes" with a short reason and the advice to keep a copy elsewhere, in English and Russian (ADR-0287). A pool on it is created with the same typed confirmation and erase checks as on any disk (ADR-0035).
- **The idle shutdown** (`power.schedule._disk_counters`, every minute, without lsblk) counts the reads and writes of the cards that the last read of the host's state found free (`disks.free_cards`, kept in RAM by `_build_state`: GET /storage/disks, the SMART pages and the hourly SMART monitor read it), never those of the system's card.
- **The device check** `scripts/dev/scenario.sh smart` expects NO_SMART of a listed card and a 400 for its self-test.
- Tests: `supervisor/tests/test_mmc_data_disk.py` (the W3 now and before, the uses that hide a card, what cannot be read, the boot and RPMB areas, SMART, spin-down, the idle shutdown), the changed cases of `test_disk_safety.py`, `test_pool_import.py` and `test_health_smart.py`, the smart scenario's in `test_dev_scenarios.py`; `webui/tests/storage.spec.ts` and `webui/tests/health.spec.ts`.

## Consequences

- On the W3 the SD card `mmcblk1` is a data disk, and the eMMC `mmcblk0` with the system stays hidden; the system's own SD card or eMMC stays hidden on every board, the BPI-F3's included.
- A free eMMC is a data disk too. On a host whose eMMC keeps another system that nothing mounts (the W3 before its move, with UGREEN's firmware and loader), that eMMC is now offered: it holds data, so a pool on it needs the erase confirmed and the name typed again. There the board started from the SD card's loader, not the eMMC's (ADR-0470: the RK3588's boot ROM starts the loader of a card in the slot before the eMMC's; `ubootpart=` named the SD partition).
- UNOS cannot see a bootloader that a card keeps in raw sectors outside its partitions, which no mount and no `ubootpart=` name. A pool on a free card erases its loader: an SD card with one, which the boot ROM would start before the eMMC, then no longer starts the board, which falls back to the eMMC. The case to guard is an eMMC whose loader starts the board while the system lives on another disk (NVMe, USB) that `ubootpart=` does not name on the eMMC: that eMMC looks free, and a pool on it would leave the board without a loader. Such a card is offered all the same; the docs (`docs/USER_GUIDE.md`, `docs/INSTALL.md`) tell the user to make no pool of the card whose loader starts the board. Reading each card's first sectors for the loaders of each vendor (Rockchip's `RKNS` at sector 64 among them) is left for later.
- A card mounted outside a pool is hidden, not listed as in use like a SATA disk.
- The SD card standby of ADR-0471 hides its card from the disk list once the card holds the standby (by its labels and folder, and it is mounted under `/mnt/.unos-standby`, which hides it here too); "Remove the standby" wipes the card and gives it back to this offering.
- GET /health/disks, the SMART endpoints and the monitor now read the host's mount table, swaps and command line with lsblk, to tell which card holds the system.
- What ADR-0072, ADR-0283 and ADR-0378 say of SD cards and eMMC, that every `mmcblk` device is a boot device that `HIDDEN_DISK_PREFIXES` hides, no longer holds; their decisions hold otherwise: SMART only on the Storage page's disks (an SD card or eMMC among them, which has none), a filesystem found on the disks a pool only from data disks, and no idle timeout for a boot device (400) or for a disk that cannot have one (409, an SD card or eMMC among them).
